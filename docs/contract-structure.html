spec-flow / contract structure

The contract structure

A fixed, host-agnostic scaffold of headed sections — bullets and tables, never prose. The same shape whether the body lives in a .docs/ file or a Linear ticket.

Six headed sections. Every contract carries the same six: What we're doing, Why, Approach, Out of scope, Done when, and Open questions. The shape is fixed and host-agnostic. Done when is load-bearing — it's what the close gate (and the contract-verifier agent) checks the change against.

The scaffold

This is the literal scaffold spec-flow:start produces (the frontmatter block is file-host only — Linear has its own metadata):

---
status: active
topic: <slug>
started: YYYY-MM-DD
---

# <Goal in plain language, one line>

## What we're doing
- <one or two bullets describing the change>

## Why
- <one or two bullets — the trigger or motivation>

## Approach
- <larger strokes only — architecture, libraries, integration points>
- <no task list, no enumeration>

## Out of scope
- <explicit non-goals — fence against the AI "helpfully" expanding>

## Done when
- <observable outcome — what the user/system can now do>
- <visible state change — file exists, command works, endpoint returns>
- <verification gate — "tests pass", or "manual smoke: X">

## Open questions
- <things deferred to during implementation — these inform cadence>

The six sections

1 · What we're doing

one or two bullets

The change in plain language. The shared starting point.

2 · Why

one or two bullets

The trigger or motivation — "the reason future-you cares."

3 · Approach

bullets · larger strokes only

Architecture, libraries, integration points. Explicitly no task list, no enumeration — if a task list is emerging, it belongs in implementation, not in the contract. This is also the section the optional debate fork feeds, and the section close compares against the shipped code to find decisions worth migrating to ndr atoms.

4 · Out of scope

explicit non-goals

A fence against the AI "helpfully" expanding the change. During implementation this is load-bearing: a tempting addition must be proposed as an amendment, not just added.

5 · Done when close gate

2–4 bullets · observable, not procedural · bullets not checkboxes

What is visibly different when the change ships — "command returns the head atom", not "implement walk function in reader.py". Checkboxes are avoided deliberately because they invite task-list creep. This section is the first gate of the close skill: each bullet is checked met / not met / drifted, and an unmet bullet halts the close. Thin drafting here means a fuzzy close later.

6 · Open questions

deferred items

Things pushed to "decide during implementation." Load-bearing in a non-obvious way: their presence or absence shapes the cadence conversation at handoff — many open questions lean toward "check in after a piece," few lean toward "all at once." A resolved open question is a candidate to promote into Approach via an amendment.

Conventions behind the shape

ConventionWhy
Bullets / lists / tables over proseShort, scannable, retrievable — matches the user's reading style.
Approach is larger strokes onlyTask lists are implementation residue, not agreement.
Done when is observable, not procedural; bullets not checkboxesGives close something concrete to verify; checkboxes invite creep.
Open questions are load-bearingThey drive the per-session cadence negotiation.
Placement signals statestatus: active while in flight; on done the file moves to .docs/archive/. The frontmatter field is informational redundancy.
No amendments without sign-offThe contract is an agreement — both parties accept before any edit lands.

What the contract deliberately leaves out

Just as important as the sections is what is excluded by design. These belong elsewhere:

  • Task list / enumerated steps — belong in implementation, not the contract.
  • System spec / "current state of the app" — the durable layer's job (README, ndr atoms, code).
  • Cross-change roadmap — spec-flow is single-change-scoped.
  • Enumerated acceptance criteria / test plan — Done when captures verification gates as observable outcomes; specific test cases stay in implementation.

The recurring idea: the contract holds the agreement (shared model), and everything that is durable knowledge or enumerated work lives somewhere else. The bullets are the residue, not the deliverable.
